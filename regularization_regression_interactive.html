Why does the penalty term help?

The cost J(θ) is made of two competing pieces: how badly we fit the data, and how big the parameters are. Gradient descent minimises their sum — so it must decide what a better fit is worth. λ is the price of coefficient size. Drag it below.

J(θ) = 12m [ ∑ i=1 m ( hθ (x(i)) − y(i) ) 2 + λ ∑ j=1 n θj2 ]
Fit term — how wrong the predictions are Penalty term — the squared L2 norm of the parameters
The sum in the penalty starts at j=1, not j=0: the intercept θ0 is not penalised. Shifting every house price up by $10k is not “complexity”, so we don’t charge for it.
Regularization parameter λ = 0 overfitting

The model never changes. It is a degree-9 polynomial at every setting of λ — we are not deleting terms, only changing what we are willing to pay for their coefficients.
