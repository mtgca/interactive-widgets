Convolution of PDFs

If independent random variables are added, their densities combine by convolution. Move the shifted PDF across the other one and watch the overlap build the density of the sum.

1. Exponential random variable

fX(x)=λe−λx,x≥0

2. Uniform random variable

fY(y)=1T,0≤y≤T

Slide, overlap, integrate

The blue window is the flipped-and-shifted density fY(z−w). Its support is always [z−T,z].

The overlap traces the density of the sum

Each value of z produces one integral value and therefore one point on the output density.

The curve rises while the moving window gains overlap with the exponential support. After z passes T, the overlap width stays equal to T but slides into the exponential tail.

Piecewise result

The integration interval is the intersection of w≥0 and z−T≤w≤z.

fZ(z)=0,z<0
fZ(z)=1−e−λzT,0≤z≤T
fZ(z)=e−λ(z−T)−e−λzT,z>T
Key idea. Convolution turns the moving overlap into the probability density of a sum of independent random variables.

Model used in the class exercise: X is exponential with rate λ, Y is uniform on [0,T], the variables are independent, and Z=X+Y.
